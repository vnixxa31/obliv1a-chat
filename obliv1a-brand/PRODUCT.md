## Platform

web

## Stack

Static HTML/CSS with Tailwind. Chosen by the user for the brand identity deliverable: no build step, opens anywhere, and the tokens port into whatever stack the real product ships on later.

## Users

Laypeople who want out of Big Tech and have no technical means to get there. They are uneasy about surveillance — they have heard "you are the product" often enough to believe it — but they will not run a home server, read a threat model, or compile anything. They are typing things they would not want permanently attached to their name: a medical worry, a legal question, a draft resignation, an argument with a partner. They have opened a privacy policy once and closed it.

Secondary: developers and privacy-literate users who audit the source and vouch for it to the first group. They are not the design target, but their approval is the first group's evidence.

## Product Purpose

Obliv1a is an open-source software provider making tools for people who want to avoid Big Tech without becoming technical. The current product is Obliv1a Chat, an AI chatbot. More products are planned; the brand must hold them.

Success is an ordinary person choosing Obliv1a over a Big Tech equivalent and not feeling like they downgraded.

## Positioning

Obliv1a hosts the service and retains nothing: no logs, no training on user conversations, no account trail. The source is open, so the claim is auditable rather than promised.

The mechanism a neighbor could not truthfully copy: **hosted convenience with deliberate amnesia.** Self-hosting projects ask the user to run infrastructure; Big Tech runs the infrastructure and keeps the memory. Obliv1a runs the infrastructure and throws the memory away. The product is defined by what it does not have.

## Operating Context

Consumer web and mobile web. Users arrive from word of mouth, a privacy-adjacent forum, or a news cycle about a data breach. They evaluate in minutes, not weeks, and they are comparing against a free product from a company they already distrust but already use. Nothing in the experience may require a terminal, a key, or a decision the user is not equipped to make.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Confirmed: an AI chatbot, hosted by Obliv1a, zero retention, open source.
- Naming architecture: **Obliv1a + descriptive suffixes.** The chatbot is **Obliv1a Chat**; future products follow the same pattern (Obliv1a Mail, Obliv1a Notes). No separate product sub-brands.
- Undecided, and not to be invented: pricing, funding model, model provenance, jurisdiction, hosting region, launch date, license identifier, user counts, benchmarks, audit or certification status.

## Brand Commitments

- The name is written **Obliv1a**, with the numeral 1, everywhere: logo, running text, domain, speech-adjacent copy. The numeral is load-bearing, not a stylization. User-confirmed.
- Etymology, user-supplied: a play on **oblivion** and **Olivia**. Both readings are live — the erasure and the human name.
- Binding visual constraints, user-supplied: the **Nous Research / Hermes Agent** visual language is the pinned reference (hypermodern, edgy). The identity is **purple** rather than blue. All colors are specified from the **Tailwind** palette.
- Open source is a brand-level commitment, not a feature line.

## Product Principles

1. **Amnesia is the product.** Every surface should make the absence of memory feel like the feature it is, not an apology for a missing one.
2. **No expertise required, ever.** If understanding a screen requires knowing what a threat model is, the screen has failed the primary user.
3. **Auditable over reassuring.** Open source means claims are checkable; prefer showing the check over repeating the promise.
4. **One brand, many tools.** The identity must stay legible when the chatbot is one of six products, so nothing may be built around chat specifically.
5. **Serious, not paranoid.** The audience is anxious already. The brand carries conviction and edge without recruiting them into fear.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The primary user is non-technical and may be older, low-vision, or on a low-end phone. Text contrast, target sizes, and plain language are product requirements, not polish. The brand's high-commitment color fields must never be the only carrier of meaning.
